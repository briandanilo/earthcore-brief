What Earthcore's fireplaces did to my home

Brian Danilo · San Luis Obispo County, CA · ~$250,000 in property damage · seeking contingency counsel

Earthcore Industries sold me four of its Isokern fireplaces for a new custom home — about $46,000 — then manufactured and installed them itself. They don't vent. Soot and combustion exhaust pour back into the house. The pictures below are what that looks like.

The short version

  • July 2023: days after move-in, soot-stained walls, tripping smoke alarms, hazardous indoor-air readings. Soot destroyed the HVAC system.
  • 2023–2024: a year of Earthcore repair attempts — burner swaps, new logs, retrofitted chimney fans. Nothing worked. Its own emails confirm the gas supply was in spec.
  • The problem is the product: open, non-sealed fireplaces that cannot be fixed. They must be cut out of the walls and replaced — $150–200k, plus remediation: ~$250,000 total. Property damage and economic loss only; no personal-injury claim.
  • 2026: Earthcore refused mediation, then defaulted on its AAA arbitration fees. Under CCP §1281.97 the claims now go to San Luis Obispo Superior Court — the complaint is drafted and ready to file, with limitations tolled and §1281.99 fees in play.
  • The file is built: 17,000+ indexed emails, a 653-event timeline, Earthcore's complete written repair history, full billing record, photos and video.
