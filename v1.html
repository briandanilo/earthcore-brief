Four defective Isokern fireplaces — seeking contingency counsel

Brian Danilo · San Luis Obispo County, California · claim v. Earthcore Industries, LLC (Jacksonville, FL)

In 2022 I built a custom home in San Luis Obispo County. Earthcore Industries sold me four of its Isokern fireplaces for about $46,000, and Earthcore itself manufactured and installed them.

They have never worked. The fireplaces do not draft: instead of pulling combustion gases up the flue, they spill soot and exhaust back into the house. Within days of moving in (July 2023) the walls were stained with soot, smoke alarms were tripping, and indoor air quality readings reached dangerous levels. The soot was pulled through and ruined my HVAC system.

Earthcore spent roughly a year on repair attempts — new burners, different logs, retrofitted chimney fans. None of it worked. Its own correspondence rules out the excuses: in October 2023 the propane supply was measured in-spec at the valve, and Earthcore's salesman confirmed in writing, “your pressure is ok … something doesn't seem right.” The units are open, non-sealed fireplaces that cannot be fixed; they have to be cut out of the walls and replaced. Repair and replacement estimates put my damages at roughly $250,000. There is no personal-injury claim — this is property damage and economic loss.

In 2026 I demanded mediation, and Earthcore refused to engage. I filed an AAA arbitration in September 2026; Earthcore then failed to pay its arbitration fees, and under California CCP §1281.97 the AAA closed the case, letting me take the claims to court. A complaint for damages is drafted for San Luis Obispo County Superior Court (contract, warranty, strict products liability, negligence; jury demand), with the limitations period tolled from the arbitration filing. The case is ready to file.

I am looking for a firm to take the case on contingency (or a hybrid arrangement). The file is unusually well-organized: a complete indexed email record with Earthcore (17,000+ emails captured, every fact cited to a dated message), a 653-event timeline, photographs, and Earthcore's entire written repair history.
