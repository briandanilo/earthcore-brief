The defect

In 2022 Earthcore sold, manufactured, and installed four Isokern fireplace systems (~$46,000) in a new custom home. The units do not draft: combustion gases spill back into the living space instead of venting. From the first days of occupancy (July 2023): soot-blackened walls, tripped smoke alarms, hazardous indoor-air readings, and soot drawn through the HVAC system, destroying it. The units are open, non-sealed designs; a year of Earthcore repair attempts (burner swaps, log changes, retrofitted chimney exhaust fans) changed nothing. The fireplaces must be removed from the structure.

Why liability is clean

  • One defendant made, sold, and installed the product. No finger-pointing between manufacturer and installer — Earthcore is both.
  • Earthcore's own writings defeat its defenses. Its current theory is a propane-supply problem; its own salesman confirmed in writing on 10/3/2023 that the measured supply pressure was in spec (“your pressure is ok … something doesn't seem right”), with pressure dropping inside Earthcore's own burner control box.
  • A year of admitted failed repairs is documented in Earthcore's own emails, followed by Earthcore going silent, denying liability, and then defaulting on its arbitration fees.
  • Litigation-conduct leverage: Earthcore recorded a mechanics lien against the home in 2024 and abandoned it (never enforced; lapsed), and its arbitration fee default carries mandatory §1281.99 consequences.
